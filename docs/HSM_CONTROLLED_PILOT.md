# Horwich St Mary’s controlled pilot protocol

## Safety rule

Ground Control runs alongside the club’s existing process until the controlled-use cycle is formally accepted. The club must retain a fallback schedule and communication route.

## Cycle 1 — Historical replay

Use completed matchweeks with known real outcomes.

Record:

- Fixture count
- Automatically scheduled fixtures
- Manually resolved fixtures
- Unresolved fixtures
- Invalid recommendations
- Correct warnings
- Missed warnings
- Overrides
- Critical/high defects
- Estimated time saved
- Differences from the schedule actually used

Minimum progression rule: no critical security/data-loss defect, no closed or clearly unsuitable pitch silently accepted, and all discrepancies explained.

## Cycle 2 — Shadow live

Use an upcoming real matchweek, but continue the normal club process as the operational source.

Compare Ground Control with the real planning process and log every defect, usability issue, data issue, training need and feature request separately. Feature requests do not automatically block the pilot.

Minimum progression rule: schedule saves reliably, reports reconcile to the final schedule, important warnings are not missed and operators can complete routine work with limited guidance.

## Cycle 3 — Controlled use

Use Ground Control as the primary planning workspace for a limited number of matchweeks while retaining a documented fallback.

Critical or high defects are fixed or explicitly accepted before the next controlled matchweek. Cosmetic work and new ideas stay in the backlog until the cycle ends.

## Cycle 4 — Sign-off

Record one of these outcomes:

- **Pass:** ready for selected external pilot clubs.
- **Conditional:** usable with clearly stated restrictions and follow-up actions.
- **Fail:** another HSM cycle is required.

A passing sign-off must name the person providing operational acceptance and link the pilot evidence to the HSM sign-off launch gate.

## Finding severity

- **Critical:** security breach, cross-club exposure, data loss, unusable core workflow or unsafe operational output.
- **High:** major scheduling/report error without a reliable workaround.
- **Medium:** material usability or data-quality problem with a workaround.
- **Low:** cosmetic issue, minor friction or improvement idea.

## Pilot change control

- Critical defects: interrupt the pilot and fix immediately.
- High operational defects: prioritise before the next controlled-use session.
- Medium/low issues: record and triage.
- Feature requests: backlog unless essential to complete the agreed pilot scope.
